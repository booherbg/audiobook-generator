# Work Queue — the library's reading list, traced and vetted

A curated, copyright-checked queue of texts to bring into the library. The organizing idea:
**start at the centerpiece (Magnifica Humanitas, 2026) and trace its references backward** —
through the lineage of Catholic social teaching, into the philosophy of technology and
humanism, back to the ancients. A main thread, with offshoots. Then two wider runs: **world
wisdom traditions** on work/machines/consciousness, and **public-domain science fiction &
free manifestos** on technology and the human future.

This is a queue, not a promise. Each item carries a **copyright verdict** because the project's
rule is firm: *reproduce only what is freely/legally shareable; never compete with commercial
works; never pirate.* Status is the first filter, thematic relevance the second.

> **How to read the status column**
> - **PD** — public domain (US, pre-1930 by the 95-year rule, or a government work). Free to reproduce.
> - **PD-work / ⚠ translation** — the *work* is public domain, but you must use a *public-domain
>   translation* (named per item). Modern scholarly translations are copyrighted — the #1 trap.
> - **Free-licensed** — explicitly released under CC / a verbatim-copy permission. Free under stated terms.
> - **Vatican / cite-source** — encyclicals: LEV claims copyright but its own policy permits
>   free full-text republication *with attribution, not sold separately* — exactly this project's posture.
> - **© In copyright** — not reproducible. May be *named + quoted briefly* (fair use) in companion notes only.
>
> ⚠ **The translation trap:** an ancient text being public domain does NOT make a given English
> edition free. Always pull from a PD-dedicated source (Gutenberg, Standard Ebooks, Wikisource,
> sacred-texts) and the specific PD translation named below — never a modern Penguin/Oxford/Harvard edition.

Status of the library today: **Magnifica Humanitas** (2026) shipped; **Rerum Novarum** (1891)
shipped (work-in-progress badge); **Laudato Si'** (2015) is the agreed next build.

### 1D. Philosophy of technology — the deep roots (almost all © — cite, don't reproduce)

Magnifica's intellectual parents on man-vs-machine. **Verdict: none can enter the pipeline — all
in copyright for decades.** Build a "further reading" appendix that names them and quotes only the
short lines Magnifica itself quotes (fair use). Two are cited *by name* in the encyclical.

- **Romano Guardini**, *The End of the Modern World* / *Letters from Lake Como* — cited ("Contemporary man has not been trained to use power well"). © to ~2038.
- **Hannah Arendt**, *The Human Condition* (1958) — the labour/work/action triad behind "dignity of work." © well past 2050.
- **Jacques Ellul**, *The Technological Society* (1954/64) — the strongest statement of "technology is never neutral." ©.
- **Lewis Mumford**, *Technics and Civilization* (1934) — machines as carriers of values. © (renewed) **until 2030 — revisit then**, the first to free up.
- **Heidegger**, *The Question Concerning Technology* (1954/77); **Josef Pieper**, *Leisure: The Basis of Culture* (1948); **Norbert Wiener**, *The Human Use of Human Beings* (1950) — all ©.

## THREAD 2 — World wisdom traditions (secondary run)

What other faith & wisdom traditions say on work, machines, humanism, and consciousness. Vetted
hard: genuine primary texts separated from romanticized/pseudo-historical claims.

**The 3 strongest, most grounded, cleanly-PD candidates** (a "World Wisdom on Work & Machines" collection):

| # | Tradition | Text | Why it's the anchor | Status — use THIS translation |
|---|-----------|------|---------------------|-------------------------------|
| ★★ | Taoist | **Zhuangzi — the "machine heart" (機心) passage** (ch. "Heaven and Earth") | **The crown jewel.** ~2,300 yrs old: "where there are ingenious contrivances, there are sure to be scheming/machine minds… pure simplicity is impaired." A meditation on how using machines reshapes the human interior — astonishingly precise for an AI collection | **PD** — James **Legge**, *Texts of Taoism*, SBE (1891), sacred-texts / ctext |
| ★★ | Jewish | **The Golem cluster — authentic sources** (*Sefer Yetzirah*; Talmud *Sanhedrin 65b*) | The deepest pre-modern reflection on humans creating an artificial servant from earth + language — and on its *limits* (the golem cannot speak). A direct mirror to the AI material | **PD** — Kalisch (1877) / Westcott (1887) for Sefer Yetzirah; Rodkinson (1903) for the Talmud passage. **Present the Prague/Maharal version honestly as 1909 literature, not tradition** |
| ★★ | Hindu | **Bhagavad Gita on *karma yoga*** (ch. 2–4) | The great non-Western philosophy of **work without attachment to its fruits** ("a right to action, but never to its fruits") — a profound alternative framing of labour and dignity | **PD** — Edwin **Arnold**, *The Song Celestial* (1885) |

**Strong runners-up:**
- **Liezi — the Yan Shi automaton** (Lionel Giles, 1912, **PD**): the *narrative* automaton tale (a mechanical man so lifelike the king mistakes it for human) that pairs with the Zhuangzi's *philosophical* warning.
- **Upanishads** on consciousness / Atman (Max **Müller**, SBE, 1879/84, **PD**); **Dhammapada** on mind as forerunner of all things (Müller, 1881, **PD**, Gutenberg #2017).
- **Pirkei Avot** on the dignity of labour ("Great is work, for it honours the worker"; Gorfinkle 1913, **PD**, Gutenberg #8547).
- **Ikhwan al-Safa**, *The Case of the Animals versus Man* (10th c.) — a fable interrogating human supremacy (PD-work; ⚠ only the loose Platts/Eastwick 1869 translation is PD).
- **al-Jazari**, *Book of Ingenious Mechanical Devices* (1206) — the great medieval automata treatise; the **manuscript miniatures are PD on Wikimedia** (usable as art), but no PD English translation exists → use as visuals + narrated commentary, not recited text.

**⚠ Vetting flags — do NOT use / handle with care:**
- **"Ancient Indian flying machines / Vaimanika Shastra"** — **debunked 20th-century pseudo-history** (composed 1918–23; a 1974 IISc study found the craft couldn't fly). The classic "ancient aliens" red flag. **Exclude.**
- The dramatic **"2,500-year-old Indian killer robots"** headlines — there *is* a real legend (the *bhuta-vahana-yantra* guarding the Buddha's relics, in the Burmese Pali *Lokapannatti*), but it's later than billed, **Buddhist not Hindu**, and the text itself credits *Greek* ("Yavana") engineers. Present soberly, citing the real scholarship (Signe Cohen *I, Yantra* 2023; Adrienne Mayor *Gods and Robots* 2018 — both ©, for citation not reproduction).
- **Golem of Prague / Maharal** — the famous version is Rabbi Yudl Rosenberg's **1909 literary invention**, not tradition. Use, but label honestly.

## Cross-cutting cautions (the project's rule, restated)

- **Verify copyright per item, every time.** A public-domain *work* ≠ a public-domain *edition/translation*. Pull from Gutenberg / Standard Ebooks / Wikisource / sacred-texts and the named PD translation.
- **The Vatican line:** reproduce encyclicals with attribution to vatican.va, never sold separately — the posture the project already holds.
- **When unsure, it's a "VERIFY," not a "yes."** Flag it, link out, don't host.
- **Honesty in curation:** present later folklore (Golem of Prague, the "ancient robot" headlines) as what it is; exclude debunked pseudo-history (Vimanas).

## Deferred design note: the commentator's persona (see backlog)

A separate design proposal recommends giving the AI director's-commentary a **stated lens** — "the
voice in the margin," a Stoic-humanist reader (Montaigne/Aurelius + the deep-time sensibility of
Stephenson/Sagan/Le Guin, Asimov's "the laws are about the humans," a low simmer of Pratchett).
The principle: *a declared lens is more honest than a hidden default* — it converts an invisible
training-set bias into a disclosed one the reader can calibrate against. The hard line: **the voice
may describe the lens it looks through; it may never invent the eye** (no fabricated name,
biography, body, or feelings — that would be the very "AI pretending" failure the project avoids).
Full proposal + paste-ready persona charter is tracked in BACKLOG.md. *(Text/playbook change, zero new audio.)*
