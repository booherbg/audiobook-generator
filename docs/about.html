About this project

This is a small, free audiobook library. Each title is a freely-shared text, narrated by a local text-to-speech engine and played from plain static files — no account, no tracking, no paywall.

It was built as a collaboration between a human (Blaine, who likes to tinker) and an AI (Claude). That pairing isn't incidental: the first title here is Pope Leo XIV's encyclical Magnifica Humanitas, which argues that technology should serve human dignity rather than concentrate power. Making a careful, freely-accessible edition of that text together felt like a small, honest instance of the idea.

Alongside it is Rerum Novarum (Leo XIII, 1891), the encyclical that began modern Catholic social teaching and that Magnifica Humanitas explicitly carries forward — the first machine age speaking to the second. Each edition has its own narration and an optional companion of notes and context.

The narration is machine-generated and checked for clarity, but it is not a human reader and may occasionally mispronounce a name or a Latin phrase. The source text is always the authority; this is a way to listen, not a substitute for the original.

The encyclical texts are © Libreria Editrice Vaticana and are reproduced here freely, with attribution and without charge, for accessible reading and study — never sold or monetized. The authoritative originals are published by the Holy See at vatican.va, to which each edition links. Rerum Novarum (1891) is also in the public domain by age. If a rights-holder ever wishes a text removed, it will be, promptly.

Built with Kokoro (text-to-speech), ffmpeg, and a few hundred lines of vanilla JavaScript.
